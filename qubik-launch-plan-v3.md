## Why this segment

At Cometlab.ai, which sells similar services in India, the businesses that came looking for this help were manufacturers and distributors ⟦one general line on what you saw; no client names or numbers⟧. I tested that against Australia:

- **Size and fit.** About 10,800 wholesalers turn over A$2–10M (ABS); I estimate 6,700 in A$3–10M. That's 16% of all wholesalers in the band, against 7% for trades (ABS), so more Meta responders are worth calling.
- **Qubik's edge.** Landed cost, stock values and import GST all land in the Xero file an accountant reads monthly, and Qubik is a Xero Platinum partner (Xero).
- **Live pressure.** The ACCC reports record stevedoring charges flowing through to importers (ACCC, Dec 2025).

Trades, pubs, labour hire and job-shop manufacturers lost on band share, crowding, margins or build risk.

## 1. Buyer and problem

**Organisation.** Buys from overseas factories, holds stock here, sells to trade or retail (hardware, electrical, lighting, auto parts, packaging, homewares, food). A$3–10M revenue, 10–40 staff. They say "we're the Australian distributor for…", not "importer-distributor".

**Decision-maker.** The owner or MD signs. The finance or office manager, or external bookkeeper, carries weight.

**The problem.** A container's costs don't arrive with the container. The supplier invoice comes first, the freight bill later, and the customs broker's invoice with duty and GST can come weeks after the stock is on the shelf. Software like Unleashed can spread these costs and even recalculate margin after sale (Unleashed), so the software isn't the gap. The gap is someone chasing three parties' paperwork, and prices only being reviewed once it's in. In Xero-only or MYOB shops it lives in a spreadsheet, if anywhere. It repeats with every container, and meanwhile stock sells at last quarter's price.

**Likely cost (estimate, replaced by real numbers in the diagnostic).** A A$6M distributor with A$3.6M of imported sales: a 5% landed-cost rise recovered three months late costs about A$45k. Two a year is about A$90k of margin, before the hours spent chasing paperwork.

**Buying triggers.** A freight or currency jump, a quarter where margin "went missing", the bookkeeper leaving, a stocktake that won't reconcile. And now: Christmas stock is landing, and orders ahead of Chinese New Year (6 Feb 2027) go to factories in November (Sino-Shipping).

**Ability to pay.** At this size, A$57–90k is a normal investment if it pays back in about a year, which the diagnostic tests. Cash is checked on the first call.

**Why Meta.** Owners use Facebook personally, so Meta reaches them; since 2025's targeting changes it can't identify them (Meta changes). The hook and form filter: only an importer recognises the problem, and dropshippers and employees fail the questions.

## 2. Offer and proof

**The Container Cost Check. A$3,500 + GST, founding-client price.** Three weeks, fixed scope. The client receives:

1. **Their last three to five shipments, costed properly** from the real documents.
2. **The gap in dollars:** true cost against system cost and actual sell price, product by product.
3. **The lag:** days from container arrival to fully costed, and who spends how long chasing paperwork.
4. **A fixed-price plan** to have costs worked out the day the paperwork lands, with payback.
5. **A one-hour sit-down with the owner** and a short report.

They provide read-only Xero access, recent shipment documents and two hours of staff time.

**On price.** A "was A$7,500" claim must reflect a price actually charged (ACCC), so I'd say "A$3,500 for our first ten clients, A$7,500 after", once Qubik commits.

**Proof at launch, all real.** Xero Platinum partner since 2013, CA ANZ and CPA memberships (Xero) ⟦confirm⟧. The automation specialist, named, with something they've built ⟦ask Qubik⟧. A sample report on labelled dummy data. And the founding-client deal: ten clients get the price in exchange for anonymised before-and-after results, so proof is built, not invented.

**Bridge to implementation.** The diagnostic's dollar figure becomes the business case for a build already scoped and priced in the report, with the A$3,500 credited if they proceed within 30 days. A typical build captures supplier, freight and broker documents from the inbox, matches them to the shipment, posts landed cost to Unleashed, Cin7 or Xero, and lists products now below target margin each week. Staff approve anything uncertain. Systems and supplier count set where it lands in A$57–90k.

## 4. Qualification, nurture and handover

**Scoring.** At four to six leads a human calls everyone; tiers set priority. Gates: imports, revenue A$2M+, role not "Other". **Qualified:** passes and says "Yes, soon" or "Probably". **Review:** passes but says "Not now", or is over A$10M. **Nurture:** fails a gate; monthly email. At volume it becomes a points score fed back to Meta.

**Six events from form to sales:**

| # | Event | What happens | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lead arrives | In the CRM within a minute: cleaned, de-duplicated, tiered, consent and source ad recorded. | Automation |
| 2 | Reply within 5 minutes | SMS and email from a named Qubik accountant with a booking link. Review tier: a same-day call instead. | Automation; copy by Marketing |
| 3 | Call within 15 minutes (business hours) | Check the uncertain answers, book them on the spot. | Sales |
| 4 | Booked | Booking stops every chaser; two pre-call questions (what they sell, when the last container landed). Unbooked: email day 1, SMS day 3, email day 6, then monthly. | Automation |
| 5 | Reminders and no-shows | Email the day before, SMS two hours before, both with a reschedule link. No-show: SMS after 10 minutes, email next day, call day 3, then monthly. | Automation; call by Sales |
| 6 | Handover | One-page brief: answers, ad seen, all messages. Sales accepts or rejects within a business day, with a reason I review weekly. | Sales, with Marketing |

**Rules.** *Duplicates:* matched on mobile, email or company; merged, never restarted; Qubik clients go to their accountant. *Replies:* pause automation; a person answers within two business hours. *Opt-outs:* STOP or unsubscribe removes them everywhere immediately (law allows five working days, ACMA); every message identifies Qubik. *Uncertain answers:* never auto-rejected. *Escalation:* complaints, privacy, price negotiation or automation failures go to a named person.

**Ownership.** Marketing (me): ads, form, tiers, copy, nurture, reporting. Automation (Qubik's specialist and IT): CRM, routing, messaging, consent records. Sales (Qubik): calls, lead acceptance, closing.

## 5. Economics and downside

The brief's rates, all applied to qualified leads, with A$73.5k as the midpoint implementation:

| | A$150 CPL | A$225 CPL |
|---|---|---|
| Leads (A$900 ÷ CPL) | 6 | 4 |
| Qualified (30%) | 1.8 | 1.2 |
| Attended (70% of qualified) | 1.26 | 0.84 |
| Diagnostics (50% of qualified) | 0.9 = A$3,150 | 0.6 = A$2,100 |
| Implementations (25% of qualified) | 0.45 = A$33,075 | 0.3 = A$22,050 |
| **Gross attributed revenue** | **A$36,225** | **A$24,150** |

At A$57k–90k implementations the totals range A$28,800–43,650 and A$19,200–29,100.

**Reading it straight.** A$900 buys four to six leads, so the real result is nothing or one sale; it tests the funnel. The rates are generous: 7 in 10 attendees buying a diagnostic, and half of those going on to build. And revenue is almost all implementation, arriving two to four months later.

**The plan I'd hold us to:**

| By | Expect | If not |
|---|---|---|
| A$450 spent | At least 2 leads, at least 1 qualified | Change the hook |
| A$900 spent | CPL at or under A$225, at least 30% qualified, at least 1 call booked | See below |
| Day 30 | At least 1 diagnostic sold | Proof and offer |

**Main risk: lead quality, not lead cost.** Each lead is worth about A$6,000 at the brief's rates, so even a A$600 CPL works if 30% qualify. A form full of people who'll never buy drags qualification to 10–15%, and everything downstream shrinks.

**What I'd change first: the filter.** If qualification is low, tighten the callout ("for distributors who import by the container") and move revenue to the first question. Only then, in order: the hook and video if CPL is high, response speed if the right people don't book, proof and terms if they book but don't buy.
