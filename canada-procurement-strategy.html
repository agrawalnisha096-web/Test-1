Canada's US-supplier restrictions: the opening for Trillet

What Canada has actually changed in public procurement, why an Australian vendor can bid where US voice AI vendors increasingly cannot, what we must confirm first, and the content plan to capture demand.

Summary

The claim is partly true, and the opportunity is real

The claimOverstated

There is no national ban on all US companies and no healthcare-specific ban. Federal and provincial rules now restrict US suppliers, and Ontario's rule covers hospitals.

Why Trillet qualifiesCPTPP, not the Commonwealth

Australia and Canada share a trade agreement with a procurement chapter. Ontario's ban only targets US-headquartered firms.

RecommendationPursue, after four checks

Lead with Ontario hospitals and provincial agencies. Confirm domicile, sub-processors, Canadian hosting and certifications before any public claim.

How it escalated

  1. Mar 4, 2025Ontario bars US businesses from provincial procurement.
  2. Dec 16, 2025Federal Buy Canadian and reciprocal procurement policies take effect.
  3. Apr 13, 2026Buy Ontario Procurement Directive in force across the broader public sector.
  4. Jun 15, 2026Federal strategic-procurement threshold drops to $5M.
  5. Sep 8, 2026Canada's counter-tariffs start. The US orders Canadian goods off federal purchasing schedules.
  6. Sep 16, 2026The White House widens this into a review of Canadian access to US civilian procurement. Canadian trade experts urge sector-specific bans on US bidders in response.
  7. Oct 1, 2026New Brunswick's $5M+ ban on US goods and services begins.

The direction of travel is toward more restriction, and public-sector buyers are actively looking for vendors that are not American.

Eligibility

Why an Australian vendor can bid

The Commonwealth gives no procurement rights. The real basis is trade law:

  • CPTPP. Australia and Canada are both members, and the agreement has a government procurement chapter. All provinces took sub-central commitments comparable to the WTO GPA.
  • WTO GPA. Australia joined in 2019, which gives a second route for covered procurements.
  • Ontario's test is US-specific. It excludes US-headquartered businesses only. An Australian-headquartered vendor is not caught.

Limits to state plainly

  • Trillet is eligible to bid. Federally, Canadian suppliers still keep the 10% price advantage and content points.
  • CPTPP excludes hospital purchasing in some provinces, and municipalities. Only listed services are covered. Thresholds apply: about $237,700 federally and $649,100 for sub-central entities.
  • Ontario is the cleanest opening, because hospitals there must exclude US vendors while an Australian one is unaffected.

Go to market

How to market it

Positioning

"Australian-owned voice AI agents, eligible where US vendors are restricted, deployed inside your Canadian environment." Keep every claim factual and neutral toward the US. Use "eligible under Buy Ontario" and "CPTPP-partner supplier". Leave the Commonwealth out of the argument.

Moves

  • Get Canadian-supplier status. The federal definition only requires a permanent place of business in Canada, with no minimum headcount. A small Canadian entity, or a partnership with a Canadian systems integrator or sovereign cloud, unlocks the 10% advantage and content points.
  • Register on the procurement portals: CanadaBuys, Supply Ontario, MERX, BC Bid and SEAO (Quebec).
  • Use Australian export support. Talk to Austrade and apply for the Export Market Development Grant to part-fund market entry.
  • Build a proof point. One Ontario hospital pilot with published call-volume and wait-time results will carry more weight than any content.
  • Timing. Coverage is at a peak with the September escalation and New Brunswick's Oct 1 start. The first posts should ship within weeks.
