## 1. Marketing to medium and large companies (dashboards and automation)

*My assumption: this means businesses of roughly A$20M+ turnover or 50+ staff, buying management dashboards plus the automation that feeds them. Please correct me if the call meant something different.*

**Who buys.** The CFO or finance manager, often with the COO. The CEO signs off larger projects. They rarely buy from an ad. They buy from someone they trust, after seeing something working.

**The hook.** *"Your numbers on Monday morning, not at month end."* Most companies this size have Xero or an ERP, a CRM, and spreadsheets in between. Finance spends the first week of every month rebuilding the same reports, so decisions are made on numbers already three or four weeks old. The pitch is one dashboard of cash, margin, sales pipeline and stock, refreshed automatically from the systems they already own, built by accountants who know what the numbers should say.

**The offer.** A fixed-price *Dashboard Pilot*. We connect two or three of their systems and build one live management dashboard in about four weeks. The pilot price is set with Qubik, and it's credited against the full build. Afterwards a monthly support retainer keeps the dashboard and its automations running. That gives Qubik recurring revenue, not one-off projects.

**Channels (Meta is the wrong main channel for this group):**
1. **Account-based outreach.** 30–50 named companies a month, found through LinkedIn Sales Navigator and industry lists. Each gets a personal message, plus a 90-second video showing a sample dashboard for their industry. Dominic sends it from his own profile.
2. **LinkedIn ads,** targeted by job title (CFO, Finance Manager, COO) and company size, which Meta can't do. Small budget, aimed at the named accounts.
3. **Partners.** Xero and ERP consultants, business bankers, and the industry associations Qubik is already part of. They meet these companies first.
4. **Small-room events.** A breakfast for 10–15 CFOs in Brisbane, "What your month-end close should look like in 2027", with a live demo.
5. **Existing clients.** Qubik's larger clients are the first pilots and the first case studies.

**What to measure:** accounts contacted, conversations, demos given, pilots sold. The cycle is longer (60–120 days), so these deals show up mostly after day 30.

## 2. If 100 leads produced no paying clients

First, the plan shouldn't let it reach 100. At A$150–225 per lead, that's A$15,000–22,500 of spend, and the day-14 and day-30 stop rules pause spending long before then. But if 100 leads did arrive with no paying client, I'd work out where in the funnel they stopped, because each stage points to a different fix:

| Where they stopped | Likely cause | What I'd do |
|---|---|---|
| Few qualified (under 20 of 100) | Wrong people: the targeting or hook attracts small businesses and tyre-kickers | Tighten the callout and form, raise the revenue gate, change the hook; check how many leads were outside the target range |
| Qualified, but few meetings (under 50% held) | Too slow, or a weak reason to talk | Measure response time; test a call within 5 minutes; give the call a clearer payoff ("you'll leave with your top 3 automation opportunities") |
| Meetings held, no diagnostic sold | Offer, price or proof | Listen to call recordings; interview 5–10 people who didn't buy; test a lower-risk entry (shorter assessment, money-back if we find no saving above the fee, or the fee credited in full) |
| Diagnostics sold, no build | The diagnostic doesn't make the business case | Check the diagnostic reports: is the dollar figure big enough, and is the build scope priced to match? |

Each of these goes in front of Dominic with numbers, not opinions. If the gap is still unexplained after the fixes, the honest conclusion is that this segment isn't buying through this channel. Then we switch to the reactivated leads, referrals and the mid/large pipeline rather than keep spending.

## 3. Weekly report

Every Monday, from the CRM, with one page of commentary covering what worked, what didn't, and next week's changes.

| Metric | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Spend | Ad spend plus tools for the week (A$, ex GST) | Meta/Google invoices, tool bills |
| Leads | New contacts entering the pipeline, by source (Meta, reactivation, referral, outbound) | CRM |
| Qualified leads | Passed the gates: right size, a decision-maker, a real need | CRM stage, set by sales |
| Cost per qualified lead | Paid spend ÷ qualified leads from paid sources | Calculated |
| Meetings held | Discovery calls that actually happened (no-shows excluded) | Calendar and CRM |
| Proposals | Diagnostic or pilot proposals sent, with value | CRM |
| Paid clients | Signed and invoiced (diagnostics and builds counted separately) | CRM, Xero |
| Collected revenue | Cash received this week (not invoiced or booked) | Xero |
| CAC | All acquisition costs (ad spend, tools, my fees) ÷ new paying clients; shown weekly and as a running total, because single weeks swing | Calculated |

Every lead keeps its first source, so a client who first came from the reactivated list but signed after seeing an ad is still counted as reactivation. The report shows the current week, the previous week, the total to date, and a simple funnel from leads to collected revenue.

## 5. How I'd propose to be measured and paid (for discussion)

I'd like most of my upside tied to cash Qubik actually collects, with a small base to cover the fixed setup and weekly work.

| Component | Proposal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Base fee | ⟦A$X per month⟧ for setup, campaigns, reactivation and reporting | Covers the hours regardless of sales-cycle timing |
| Qualified meeting held | ⟦A$X⟧ per meeting accepted by sales and actually held | Rewards quality, not volume; sales decides what counts |
| Diagnostic revenue | ⟦X%⟧ of collected diagnostic fees | Paid only on cash collected |
| Implementation revenue | ⟦X%⟧ of collected build fees within 6 months of the lead's first contact | Ties me to the outcome that matters to Qubik |
| Retainer revenue (mid/large) | ⟦X%⟧ for the first 12 months | Rewards recurring revenue |

**Guardrails I'd suggest:**
- payment on collected cash only, refunded if the fee is refunded;
- the CRM's first-source field decides attribution;
- reactivated leads earn a lower percentage than new leads, because Qubik already owns them;
- a review at day 30 and day 90, and either side can adjust the terms.

## Reactivating the 3,800 existing leads

*I couldn't open the Facebook reel from my side, because the link needs a login. I'll fit the reel's message into the steps below once I've seen it. I've also assumed these are past enquiries and contacts of Qubik's; the consent and age of each record decide what we're allowed to do.*

### Next steps

1. **Export and audit (days 1–3).**
   - Find where each lead came from, when, and what consent was given.
   - Remove duplicates and bounced or invalid emails.
   - Check mobile numbers against the Do Not Call Register before any calls.
2. **Segment (days 3–5).**
   - **A: recent and good fit** (last 2 years, a business, likely A$3M+). Called by a person.
   - **B: older but a business.** Email and SMS, plus Meta retargeting.
   - **C: individuals, very old, or no consent.** Meta custom audience only, or excluded.
3. **Consent rule.** Email and SMS go only to people with express or clearly inferred consent, such as a past enquiry or business relationship. Every message identifies Qubik and has a working unsubscribe. Anyone uncertain goes into the Meta audience (allowed under an updated privacy policy) rather than the inbox. *Qubik should confirm this with its privacy adviser.*
4. **Message.** Short and personal from Dominic: "We've started helping businesses like yours automate the paperwork that slows them down. Here's a 60-second video [the reel]. Want a free 30-minute look at what's worth automating?" Then a reply, a booking link, or a call from the team.
5. **Send in waves.** Wave 1 to about 600 of segment A and the best of B (days 8–14), so we learn before using the whole list. Waves 2–3 to the remainder (days 15–30), using the winning message.
6. **Retarget everyone contactable** on Meta with the reel and the offer for 30 days. People who watch it get a second, offer-led ad.
7. **Follow up.** Two follow-ups over 10 days, then a monthly useful email (an automation example or a Xero tip) for anyone who didn't respond.
