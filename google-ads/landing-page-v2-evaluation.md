# Landing page v2: evaluation and blueprint

**Page:** qubik.com.au/automation/ (Google Ads, "Process automation" ad groups)
**Goal for v2:** explain automation to people who are new to it, build enough trust to book a call, and leave the specifics (price, timeline, exact solution) for that call.

---

## 1. Summary

1. **Many visitors don't know what automation would look like in their business.** They search "business process automation" or "automation consultant" because something hurts, not because they've chosen a solution. The v1 page assumes they already understand, then asks for nine fields before explaining anything.
2. **v2 should teach before it asks.** Plain-English explanation, before-and-after examples from a normal business day, and "what automation isn't" to lower the fear of the unknown.
3. **Pricing should explain how it works, not quote figures.** Qubik will sell fixed-price builds, phased builds, monthly support and dashboard pilots. Locking the page to one assessment price and a A$57–90k build range scares off early-stage visitors and boxes in the sales call.
4. **Leave the "how much" and "how long" open, on purpose.** Say clearly what it depends on, and that the call is where it gets worked out. That gives people a reason to call and gives sales room to tailor.
5. **Lower the commitment of the first step.** Replace the nine-field form with a three-step form that starts with an easy question ("What's taking up your team's time?") and asks for contact details last.
6. **Don't plan on A/B testing yet.** At about 100–150 clicks and 5–12 leads a month, a split test would take many months to reach a reliable result. Build v2 on proven practice, then judge it on lead quality from call notes.

## 2. Who lands on this page

| Ad group | What they typed | What they know | What they need from the page |
|---|---|---|---|
| AG1 Automation consultant & services | automation consultant, workflow automation services | Know they want help, not sure what's possible | Proof Qubik understands their kind of business; what the help looks like |
| AG2 Business process automation | business process automation, ai workflow automation | Heard the term, often vague on what it means in practice | A plain explanation and real examples; reassurance it's not a big IT project |

Most are **problem-aware** (they feel the pain: manual data entry, slow invoicing, staff stretched) but only partly **solution-aware**. Copywriting practice (Eugene Schwartz's awareness levels) says these visitors need a longer page that explains the problem and solution before the offer. Visitors who already know what they want simply scroll straight to the form, so a longer page costs them nothing.

The likely visitor is an owner, GM or finance/operations manager at a business with A$3–10M revenue and 10–50 staff. Not technical, short on time, wary of being sold expensive software.

## 3. Evaluation of v1

### Keep
- **Headline matches the search.** "Business process automation for growing Australian businesses" repeats what they typed. Keep it.
- **The accounting-firm angle.** "We start with your numbers" is a real difference from tech agencies. Keep it, and move it higher.
- **The invoice log.** A concrete picture of what automation actually does. Keep it, and add more of this kind of example.
- **How it works, FAQ, phone number in the header.**

### Change

| Issue | Why it hurts | v2 fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Automation" is never explained | New visitors can't picture it, so they can't judge if it's for them | "Automation in plain English" section plus before/after examples |
| Nine-field form in the hero, before any explanation | Asks for commitment before trust. Cold search traffic converts better with a lighter first ask. | Three-step form: easy question first, contact details last |
| Fixed assessment price (A$3,500 / A$7,500) and build range (A$57–90k) | A big number with no context makes first-time visitors leave; it also commits Qubik to one pricing model | "How pricing works" section: the models, what drives cost, and "you'll know the price before any work starts" |
| Answers every question with a specific (3 weeks, 6–10 weeks, A$ figures) | Leaves little to talk about; sets promises sales may not want to keep | Answer the *what* and *why* fully; explain what the *how much* and *how long* depend on, then point to the call |
| Proof is one quote about Dominic, not about automation | Doesn't show automation results; the strongest trust signals (Xero partner, years in business) are text only | Xero partner badge, Dominic's photo, more client quotes, a slot for the first automation case study |
| One way to convert (book a call) | Some people would rather phone, or aren't ready yet | Keep the call form as the main ask; add a click-to-call button on mobile |
| Nothing says what happens on the call | People hesitate to book something vague that might be a sales pitch | "What happens on the call" list: four concrete things they'll leave with |

## 4. What the research says

| Finding | Strength of evidence | What it means for v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Professional services landing pages convert at a median of about 6.1%; the top quarter reach 14% or more (Unbounce 2024, 41,000 pages) | Good (large dataset) | A realistic target is 6–10%. The current plan assumes 5–8%. |
| Plain, simple copy converts better than complex copy (Unbounce analysis, as reported by secondary sites) | Directional | Write for a busy owner, not an IT manager. Short sentences, no jargon. |
| Less-aware visitors need longer pages that explain the problem first (Schwartz awareness levels) | Widely used copywriting principle | A long page is fine as long as every section answers a question the visitor has. |
| Headline should repeat the search or ad promise (message match) | Consistent across sources; also part of Google's landing page experience score | Keep the H1 aligned to each ad group's keywords. |
| Cold paid-search traffic responds better to a lighter first ask | Practitioner consensus | Make the first step of the form a single, easy question. |
| Multi-step forms often beat long single forms for lead generation (reported lifts from about 1–4% to 8%) | Anecdotal case studies, not controlled studies | Use three steps, easy questions first, contact details last. Worth it because v1 has 9 fields. |
| For custom work, publishing the pricing model and what drives cost (rather than figures or nothing) is the common middle ground | No controlled study found; consistent advice | Explain the models and cost drivers; keep figures for the call. |
| Australian automation competitors (Sandlabs, 13Labs) lead with plain-language pain points, "fixed price after scoping", "no rip and replace", and start-small messaging | Direct observation | Qubik's "start with your numbers" angle is still different; "start with one process" is now expected. |
| Google's landing page experience rewards relevant original content, transparency (business details, what happens to data) and easy navigation | Google's own guidelines | Address, ABN, privacy link and a clear explanation of what happens after the form all help the ads too. |

## 5. Blueprint for v2

Section order follows the visitor's questions: *Is this for me? → What is it? → What would it do for us? → Is it safe? → How does it work? → What does it cost? → Can I trust them? → What do I do now?*

### 1. Hero: "Is this for me?"
- **Eyebrow:** Business process automation · Australia-wide
- **H1:** Business process automation for growing Australian businesses
- **Sub:** Hand the repetitive copying, checking and chasing to software that works inside the systems you already use, so your team can get on with the work that needs them.
- **Three short proof points:** Accountants since 2007 · Xero partner since 2013 · Your data stays in your systems
- **Form step 1 (in the hero card):** *"What's taking up your team's time?"* with tap-to-select options: supplier invoices and bills · customer orders · reporting and month-end · customer enquiries · data entry between systems · not sure yet. Button: **Next**.
- **Under the card:** "Free 30-minute call · No obligation · We'll tell you honestly if it's not worth it"

### 2. Automation in plain English: "What is it?"
> **What is business process automation?**
> Most businesses have jobs that follow the same steps every time: read an invoice, type it into Xero, check it against the order, email someone if it doesn't match. Automation is software that does those steps for you, inside the systems you already use. Your team stays in charge: anything unusual is sent to a person to decide.

Then three short columns: **It reads** (emails, PDFs, forms) · **It checks** (against orders, price lists, rules) · **It acts** (posts to Xero, updates your systems, sends the email), plus a line underneath: *"and asks a person when it isn't sure."*

### 3. Before and after: "What would it do for us?"
A table or set of cards, one per common process. Describe the work, not invented savings:

| Process | Today | With automation |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier invoices | Someone opens each email, types the invoice into Xero, checks it against the order | Invoices are read as they arrive, matched to the order and posted. Only mismatches reach a person. |
| Customer orders | Orders arrive by email or PDF and are re-keyed into your system | Orders are drafted into your system automatically, ready for a quick check |
| Month-end reporting | Someone exports, copies and formats spreadsheets for days | Margin, cash and stock numbers update themselves every week |
| Customer enquiries | The same questions answered by hand, every day | Routine questions answered straight away; anything unusual goes to your team |

Keep the invoice log visual here.

### 4. Signs you're ready: self-qualification
> **You'll probably get value from automation if…**
> - someone on your team spends hours a week typing the same information into different systems;
> - invoices, orders or reports pile up when one person is away;
> - you're hiring admin staff just to keep up with growth;
> - you only find out about problems (margins, overdue payments, stock) at month-end.
>
> *Not sure? That's what the free call is for.*

### 5. What automation isn't: removing the fear
Four short myth-busters:
- **It isn't new software to learn.** We build inside Xero and the tools you already use.
- **It isn't replacing your team.** It takes the repetitive work so they can focus on customers and decisions.
- **It isn't a chatbot guessing answers.** It follows your rules and sends anything uncertain to a person.
- **It isn't a big-bang IT project.** Most businesses start with one process and expand when it pays back.

### 6. Why an accountant-led team: the difference
Keep the v1 section ("Most automation firms start with the technology. We start with your numbers…"). Move it up to here, straight after the fears are dealt with.

### 7. How working with us works: the steps, with the details left open
1. **Free 30-minute call.** We listen to how your business runs and where the time goes.
2. **Assessment.** We map the processes worth automating, measure what they cost you today, and give you a plan with the payback in dollars.
3. **Build.** We automate the first process inside your existing systems and test it on your real data with your team.
4. **Support and grow.** We keep it running, and add the next process when you're ready.

Then a box headed **"What you'll get from the free call"**:
- an honest view of whether automation is worth it for your business;
- the two or three processes most likely to pay back first;
- a rough idea of what it would involve, and what it depends on;
- no obligation, and no hard sell.

No durations here. The timeline depends on the business, and that's a call conversation.

### 8. How pricing works: open, but transparent
> **How pricing works**
> Every business runs differently, so we don't sell packages. Here's how it works:
> - **The assessment** is a fixed fee, agreed upfront. If you go ahead with a build, it's credited towards it.
> - **Builds** are priced in the way that suits you: a fixed project price, staged in phases, or spread as a monthly fee.
> - **Ongoing support** is optional and monthly, so your automations keep running as your business changes.
>
> **What affects the cost:** how many processes and systems are involved, how much volume runs through them, and how many exceptions need a person.
>
> **You'll always know the price before any work starts.**

Qubik should decide whether to show the assessment fee (see section 7 of this document).

### 9. Proof: "Can I trust them?"
- Xero partner badge and "Accountants since 2007" as visual badges, not just text.
- A photo of Dominic with a short note in his own words ("Why we started doing automation").
- Two or three client quotes. The current one is fine; add any that mention time saved, systems or reporting.
- A reserved slot for the first automation case study once a client agrees.
- A small "Based in Strathpine, QLD · working Australia-wide" line with a map pin.

### 10. FAQ: answer the beginner's questions
- We're not a tech business. Is this for us?
- How do we know what's worth automating? *(That's what the call and assessment find.)*
- Will it work with our software? *(Xero, MYOB, and most tools with email or an online login. We'll check on the call.)*
- Do we need IT staff to run it? *(No.)*
- What happens if something goes wrong? *(Anything uncertain goes to a person; support covers fixes.)*
- Is our data safe, and is AI involved? *(Data stays in your systems; AI is used only for reading documents and drafting, with a person in control.)*
- What does it cost? *(Point to "How pricing works".)*
- How long does it take? *(Depends on the process; the assessment gives you a timeline before you commit.)*
- What happens on the call?

### 11. Final call to action
**See what's worth automating in your business.** Free 30-minute call, no obligation. Button plus the phone number.

### 12. Sticky mobile bar
A slim bar fixed to the bottom on phones: **Call** · **Book a free call**. It appears after the first scroll.

## 6. The three-step form

| Step | Question | Fields | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (in hero) | What's taking up your team's time? | Tap-to-select tiles (one or more) | Easy, no personal details, gets them started |
| 2 | Tell us about your business | Staff · Annual revenue · When would you want this fixed? | Qualifies the lead for sales; still nothing personal |
| 3 | Where should we call you? | Name · Company · Work email · Mobile · Consent | Asked last, once they've invested two steps |

Hidden click-ID fields stay as built. The thank-you page, redirect and tracking work the same as now.

**On Divi:** Divi's own Contact Form module can't do steps. Two options:
- **Recommended:** a custom HTML form in a Code module that collects the steps on the page, then submits into the existing Divi form fields (hidden). Same email, same redirect, nothing new to install.
- **Alternative:** a form plugin with multi-step support (WPForms Pro, Gravity Forms, Fluent Forms). More robust and stores entries, but a paid plugin and a new tool to maintain.

## 7. Decisions for Qubik before building

1. **Show the assessment fee or not?** Options: show it ("fixed fee from A$X, credited towards the build"), or show the model only. Showing it filters out tyre-kickers; hiding it gets more calls but more unqualified ones. Recommendation: hide it at launch, ask the revenue question in step 2 instead, and review after the first 20 leads.
2. **Which build pricing models will Qubik really offer?** Fixed project, phased, monthly? Only list the ones sales is happy to quote.
3. **The call promise:** is "we'll call within one business day" achievable every week?
4. **Proof:** Dominic photo, Xero badge artwork, more client quotes, any client logos allowed.
5. **AI wording:** how much to mention AI on this page (the AI consulting page will cover it in depth).

---

## 8. How to judge v2

- **Primary:** qualified leads per week and cost per qualified lead, from sales call notes (not the raw conversion rate).
- **Secondary:** form start rate (how many complete step 1), step completion rates, and conversion rate versus the 6.1% professional services median.
- **Testing:** with this traffic, change one thing at a time and compare 4-week periods rather than running split tests. Once phase 2 keywords double the traffic, the first split test worth running is "assessment fee shown vs not shown".
