## 1. Mid-market (A$3–10M): hook and marketing strategy

**Who.** Owner-led Australian businesses turning over A$3–10M, with 10–50 staff, running Xero or a similar system plus spreadsheets. The lead vertical is **wholesale distributors that import**: the earlier analysis showed about 6,700 of them in this band (ABS), and Meta estimates 550–650k Australian owners and page admins with trade-related interests. Other mid-market firms with document-heavy back offices are a secondary audience on the same campaigns.

**Two products, one entry point.** Both are sold through the same fixed-fee diagnostic, so marketing runs one funnel:
1. **Automation:** supplier and freight invoices processed automatically, with container costs as the hook.
2. **Management dashboards:** "your numbers on Monday morning, not at month end". Cash, margin, stock and sales in one view, refreshed automatically from the systems they already use.

**Hooks to test on Meta** (one problem, three costs of it):

| Hook | Line | Cost it speaks to |
|---|---|---|
| Margin | "Freight went up again. Did your prices?" | Money lost |
| Capacity | "Before you hire another person to chase supplier invoices…" | Hours and headcount |
| Visibility | "Do you know last week's margin, or will you find out at month end?" | Slow decisions; leads into dashboards |

**Meta account structure:**

| Campaign | Objective | Audience | Budget | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Prospecting · Mid-market | Leads (Instant Form, higher intent) | Australia, 30–65, trade, wholesale and supply-chain interests plus small business owners and page admins, as signals (~550–650k); Qubik clients excluded | A$900 test, then scaled on results | Find and qualify new leads |
| C2 Retargeting | Leads, or website conversions to the landing page | People who watched 50%+ of a video, engaged with the Page or Instagram, or visited the landing page (last 30 days); existing leads excluded | ~10–15% of spend | Second touch with the explainer video and the offer |
| C3 Prospecting · Trades (month 2) | Leads (Instant Form) | See section 2 | Separate budget | Second segment, kept apart so it can't distort the mid-market data |

**Conventions:**
- Campaign names follow `Segment | Objective | Audience | Test`.
- UTMs go on every ad.
- One ad set per campaign while volumes are small.
- Audience Network off.
- Lead outcomes (qualified, meeting held, sold) go back to Meta from the CRM once volume allows.

**Instant Form or landing page.** Meta's Instant Forms usually bring cheaper leads; landing pages usually bring better ones (an estimate to test). We start with Instant Forms for volume and data, then test a landing-page version once there's a baseline. Google and LinkedIn always go to the landing pages.

**Marketing assets for this segment:**
- two landing pages (automation diagnostic; management dashboard);
- four Dominic videos;
- statics and a carousel;
- a sample diagnostic report;
- a demo dashboard with a screen recording;
- a one-page sales PDF;
- lead form, SMS and email templates;
- an objection-handling sheet for sales;
- a case study template, ready for the first client.

## 2. Trades: the second target, mostly on Meta

**Why Meta.** Trade business owners are heavy Facebook users, and few search Google for "automation". The challenge is that only about 7% of trade businesses fall in the A$2–10M band (ABS), so the creative and form have to filter hard.

**Angle.** The gap between finishing a job and getting paid: quotes nobody follows up, jobs invoiced days late, and debtors chased by hand, across ServiceM8 or simPRO and Xero.

**Example hooks:** "How many quotes did you send last month that nobody followed up?" and "Jobs done Friday, invoiced Wednesday?"

**Filtering:** a form question on team size (10+ technicians), revenue, and field-service software used.

**Timing:** creative is built in weeks 3–4 and launched in month 2 as its own campaign (C3), so it doesn't muddy the mid-market test.

## 3. If 100 leads produced no paying clients

The stop rules should prevent it ever reaching 100. If it did, I'd find the stage where leads stall, because each stage has a different fix:

| Where they stopped | Likely cause | What I'd do |
|---|---|---|
| Few qualified (under 20 of 100) | Wrong people: the hook or targeting attracts small businesses and browsers | Tighten the callout and form; replace the hook; check how many leads sat outside the revenue band |
| Qualified, but under half meet | Too slow, or no strong reason to meet | Measure response time; call within 5 minutes; make the call's payoff concrete ("your top three automation opportunities") |
| Meetings held, no diagnostic sold | Offer, price or proof | Listen to recorded calls; interview 5–10 people who didn't buy; test a lower-risk entry or stronger proof (sample report, demo dashboard) |
| Diagnostics sold, no build | The diagnostic doesn't make the business case | Review the reports: is the dollar figure big enough, and does the build price match it? |

Each step is reviewed with Dominic using numbers. If the gap is still unexplained after the fixes, the segment isn't buying through this channel, and budget moves to whichever channel or segment is converting.

## 4. Weekly report

Every Monday, from the CRM, with a one-page note covering what worked, what didn't, and next week's changes.

| Metric | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Spend | Ad spend by channel (Meta, Google, LinkedIn) and tools for the week, ex GST | Ad platforms, tool invoices |
| Leads | New contacts, by channel and campaign | CRM |
| Qualified leads | Passed the gates: right size, decision-maker, real need | CRM stage, confirmed by sales |
| Cost per qualified lead | Paid spend ÷ qualified leads from paid channels, per channel | Calculated |
| Meetings held | Discovery calls that actually took place (no-shows excluded), plus the show rate | Calendar, CRM |
| Proposals | Diagnostic and build proposals sent, with value | CRM |
| Paid clients | Signed and invoiced; diagnostics and builds counted separately | CRM, Xero |
| Collected revenue | Cash received this week | Xero |
| CAC | All acquisition costs ÷ new paying clients, shown weekly and as a running total | Calculated |

Each lead keeps the channel it first came from. The report shows this week, last week and the running total, plus the funnel from leads to collected revenue for each channel.

## 5. Notes on the Google and LinkedIn tests

**Google Search (a test from about day 15):**
- **Keywords:** 26 service-intent keywords across business process automation, automation consultants, AI implementation and AI readiness assessment. That's about 990 searches a month in Australia (Keyword Planner, November 2026). Phrase and exact match only.
- **Negatives:** 138 keywords, so software shoppers, job seekers and course seekers never see the ads.
- **Forecast:** Keyword Planner forecasts about 181 clicks a month at about A$48 a day, roughly A$8 per click. That forecast was for December and in INR, so re-run it for a normal month.
- **Test plan:** A$30–48 a day for four weeks, sending people to the matching landing page.
- **Stop if:** cost per qualified lead stays above A$1,000 after four weeks of negative-keyword clean-up.
- **Before launch:**
  - conversion tracking verified (the site has none today);
  - a Google Ads account in AUD on Brisbane time; the account used for research was in INR, and currency can't be changed later.

**LinkedIn (a test from about day 21):**
- **Audience:** owners, managing directors, finance managers and operations managers at Australian companies with 11–200 staff. LinkedIn can target by job title and company size, which Meta can't.
- **Format:** a Lead Gen Form with a document ad (the sample diagnostic report or the demo dashboard).
- **Budget:** small and fixed. LinkedIn clicks usually cost several times more than Meta's (estimate), so judge it on cost per qualified lead, not cost per lead.
- **Kill rule:** no qualified lead after the fixed budget is spent.

## 6. Optional: AEO and outbound

**AEO (answer engine optimisation), long term.** Buyers increasingly ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews questions like "how do I automate invoice processing in Xero" or "what does an automation consultant cost". Qubik's Insights page is currently empty. Over three to six months:
- 10–15 pages that answer those questions directly, with clear structure and FAQ markup;
- listings in the directories AI tools draw from (Xero's advisor directory, Clutch and similar);
- tracking of AI-referred visits, and of whether Qubik gets cited.

It costs time rather than ad spend, and it compounds.

**Outbound, as a test.**
- **Who:** 100 named A$3–10M distributors, found through LinkedIn Sales Navigator and industry lists.
- **How:** three touches each: a LinkedIn message from Dominic, an email referencing their business, and a call.
- **Compliance:** cold email must meet the Spam Act. Use business addresses that are published and relevant to the person's role, identify Qubik, and include an unsubscribe.
- **Success:** a 5%+ reply rate and 3–5 meetings from the 100.
