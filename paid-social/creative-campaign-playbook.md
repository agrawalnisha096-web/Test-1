# Qubik: creative campaign playbook for LinkedIn and Meta (layer 2)

**Written:** 15 Nov 2026. **Builds on:** `linkedin-meta-strategy.md` (the base plan: offers, audiences, budget, tracking). This file adds the more creative plays:

1. **"Qubik 500":** a hand-built list of qualified target companies, used everywhere.
2. **LinkedIn DM ads** (Conversation Ads from Dominic), plus his own manual outreach timed to the ads.
3. **Meta location plays:** industrial precincts, regional hubs, trade-show venues and Qubik's own backyard.
4. **Signal-based targeting:** companies hiring admin staff, opening new sites, or using tools we can improve on.
5. **A creative testing system:** many campaigns and concepts, a weekly cycle of new ads, and clear rules for promoting winners and killing losers.

All costs are planning estimates from public 2026 benchmarks (mostly agency and US data). Dates and platform limits marked *verify* must be checked before launch.

## 2. "Qubik 500": building the qualified list

### What qualifies a company
- Australian head office; **10–1,000+ staff** (sweet spot 30–500).
- **Multi-site or multi-team** operations (3+ venues, clinics, centres, depots, contracts or sites). Multi-site is where manual reporting hurts most, and where the dashboards show off best.
- In one of the sectors we have dashboard screens for: hospitality, healthcare and allied health (including vet and dental groups), logistics and transport, facilities services (cleaning, security, maintenance), early learning, mining and civil services, retail, aviation services.
- **Not** IT companies, software firms, consultancies or accounting firms.

### Where to find them (mostly free, public data)
| Sector | Source | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Early learning** | **ACECQA National Registers** (public, downloadable list of approved providers and their services) | Filter to providers running **3–60 services**. This gives an almost perfect list of multi-centre operators, with names and states. Best single source in the plan. |
| **Hospitality** | State **liquor licence registers** (NSW Liquor & Gaming data on data.nsw.gov.au; Queensland OLGR licence search; Victoria's VCGLR register) *(verify current formats)* | Find licensees or companies holding **3+ licences**: multi-venue groups. Add groups named in hospitality media "top groups" lists. |
| **Healthcare / allied / vet / dental** | Google Maps and Healthdirect service finder; practice-group websites ("our clinics" pages) | Groups with 3+ clinics. Vet and dental groups are a good, often-missed fit. |
| **Facilities services** | **AusTender** contract notices (public, downloadable) and state tender sites (QTenders, NSW eTendering) | Suppliers winning cleaning, security, grounds and maintenance contracts. They run many contracts, each with its own margin to track. |
| **Mining and civil services** | Tender awards (as above), Queensland Resources Council / industry association member directories, regional business directories (Mackay, Gladstone, Townsville) | Contractors and service companies with several sites. |
| **Logistics and transport** | LinkedIn Sales Navigator (industry + size + HQ in Australia); industry award and association lists | Depots, 3PLs, fleets with 50–500 staff. |
| **All sectors** | Published "fastest-growing" and "top private companies" lists (AFR, state business publications) | Fast growth means admin is falling behind. |
| **Qubik's own** | Accounting clients, past enquiries, Google Ads leads, **Dominic's LinkedIn connections export** (company names) | The warmest names. Tag them separately. |

**Tools:** LinkedIn **Sales Navigator** (≈ A$150–250/month; *verify current AU pricing*) to find the decision-makers at each company and to build lists from filters. It's optional for the paid ads alone, but needed for Dominic's own outreach.

### Tiers
| Tier | Size | Who | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | ~50 | Best fit: multi-site, 50–500 staff, a clear sector match, ideally Queensland or with a Qubik connection | Everything: ads + Conversation Ad + Dominic's personal message + a **free dashboard mock-up** (section 7) |
| **Tier 2** | ~250 | Good fit | Ads + Conversation Ad + sector content |
| **Tier 3** | ~700+ | Fits the profile | Ads only |

**List sheet columns:** company, website domain, LinkedIn company URL, sector, number of sites, staff band, state, tier, source, signal (hiring/new site/etc.), notes. I can build this template.

### Using the list
- **LinkedIn company list** (upload as CSV: company name, domain, LinkedIn URL). LinkedIn needs about **300 matched companies** before it will serve to a list *(verify)*. Combine it with the seniority/title filters from the base plan, so the ads reach the decision-makers at those companies and not their warehouse staff.
- **LinkedIn contact list:** only for people we have a real relationship with (clients, past enquiries).
- **Meta:** upload customer lists only where we hold the contact details lawfully (clients, enquiries). Company lists can't be matched on Meta, so Meta uses the **location and sector plays** in section 4 instead.
- **Privacy and Spam Act:** targeting companies with ads is fine. Avoid cold bulk email to scraped addresses. Outreach goes by LinkedIn message and phone, one-to-one and relevant to the person's role.

## 3. LinkedIn DM ads (Conversation Ads from Dominic)

### Why and where
- A Conversation Ad lands in the LinkedIn inbox as a **message from Dominic** (marked "Sponsored"), with clickable buttons that branch the conversation, like a short chat.
- Agency figures: open rates around **40–65%**, cost per send about **US$0.25–1.00** (≈ A$0.40–1.50), and LinkedIn caps how often one member receives these (about one per advertiser per month; *verify in Campaign Manager*). Treat these as directional.
- **Use only on warm or list audiences:** the Qubik 500 list, people who engaged with the ads or Dominic's posts, website visitors and Company Page followers. Sent cold to a broad audience, it's expensive spam and hurts Dominic's name.
- **Dominic must approve being the sender.** His profile (photo, headline, "About") should match the message: "Accountant · Founder of Qubik · automation and live dashboards for multi-site businesses".

### Conversation tree 1: "Sector dashboard" (main version)
> **Opening (from Dominic):** "Hi {firstName}, Dominic here, founder of Qubik in Brisbane. We're accountants who build automation and live dashboards for multi-site businesses.
>
> Quick question: at {companyName}, how long after month-end do you see profit by site?"
>
> **[ Within a week ]  [ 2–3 weeks ]  [ Honestly, longer ]  [ Not my area ]**

- **"Within a week"** → "That's better than most. Usually the next win is automating what feeds the reports: supplier bills, orders and timesheets, so the team stops re-keying. Want to see what that looks like for a business like yours?" **[ Show me an example ]** (→ the sector page) **[ Book a 30-min call ]** (→ LinkedIn form) **[ Not now ]**
- **"2–3 weeks" / "Honestly, longer"** → "You're not alone. It's the most common thing we hear. We connect your accounting, payroll and rostering into one live dashboard: profit and labour by site, every Monday, plus alerts when a site slips. You see a working prototype before you commit to anything." **[ Get the playbook ]** (→ LinkedIn form, PDF) **[ Book a free 30-min call ]** (→ LinkedIn form) **[ What does it cost? ]**
  - **"What does it cost?"** → "We don't sell packages. Scoping and a working prototype is a fixed fee agreed upfront, and the build is priced against the design you approve. Most clients start with the one or two processes that pay back fastest. Happy to give you a rough idea on a call." **[ Book a call ]** **[ Send me the playbook ]**
- **"Not my area"** → "No problem, thanks for replying. Who looks after reporting and finance systems at {companyName}?" **[ Our CFO / Finance Manager ]** **[ Our Operations lead ]** → "Thanks. Here's a short playbook you could pass on." (→ LinkedIn form)

### Other trees (for rotation, since one member gets about one a month)
| # | Tree | Opening hook | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| T2 | **Hiring signal** | "Saw {companyName} is hiring in accounts. Before you add another person to data entry, it might be worth 30 minutes to see what could be automated." | Companies on the hiring list (section 5) |
| T3 | **Founding client** | "We're taking on a small number of founding clients for our automation and dashboard work, with a fixed-fee prototype first. Is {companyName} a fit?" | Tier 1–2 *(only if the founding price is confirmed)* |
| T4 | **Roundtable invite** | "I'm hosting a small lunch for multi-site operators in Brisbane on {date}: 8 people, no pitch, comparing how everyone does weekly reporting. Would you like a seat?" | Queensland Tier 1–2 |
| T5 | **Mock-up** | "We built a sample of what a weekly profit dashboard could look like for {companyName}, using public information only. Want me to send it?" | Tier 1 only (section 7) |

### Budget and expectations
- **A$20/day** ≈ A$600/month ≈ **400–1,500 sends** depending on the bid.
- Expect about **0.5–1.5% of sends to become leads** → roughly **4–20 leads a month**. Leads from list companies are worth more than average.
- **Measure:** open rate (target ≥ 45%), button click rate (≥ 4%), lead form completion, and cost per call held.

### Dominic's own outreach (free, Tier 1 and engaged Tier 2)
- In Campaign Manager, the **Company engagement report** shows which list companies are engaging with the ads. That's the call list.
- Dominic (or Vinay drafting for him) sends **10–15 personal connection requests a day**, each referencing something real ("Saw you opened your 6th clinic in Ipswich"). No pitch in the request. Once connected, he follows up with one useful thing (the playbook, or the mock-up for Tier 1).
- **Order:** ads first (2–3 weeks), then Dominic's personal note, then a Conversation Ad only for those who didn't reply. Never two direct asks in the same week.

## 4. Meta location plays

Meta can target people **living in or recently in** a place, down to a pin with a small radius. The minimum radius is about 1–1.6 km for normal ads *(verify in Ads Manager; it's larger under special ad categories)*. Small areas mean small audiences, so **group several pins into one ad set** and give each group its own local hook.

The location narrows the audience, and the **creative names the place and the role**. That's what stops a warehouse worker from filling in the form and gets a depot owner to stop scrolling.

### L1. Industrial and logistics precincts (always on)
| City | Pins (examples) | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| Brisbane | Eagle Farm / Pinkenba, Larapinta / Heathwood, Yatala, Wacol, Brendale | "Running a depot out of {area}? Here's how to see margin per job, every week." |
| Sydney | Wetherill Park, Eastern Creek, Moorebank, Smeaton Grange | Same, with the Sydney precinct named |
| Melbourne | Dandenong South, Truganina, Laverton North, Derrimut | Same |
| Perth | Kewdale, Welshpool, Canning Vale | Same |

Ages 30–65, creative: logistics dashboard screens plus the "revenue and labour cost per job" message, and the Instant Form with the staff question (under 10 → polite end).

### L2. Regional hubs (cheaper ads, fewer competitors, strong local trust)
- **Mackay / Paget, Moranbah, Emerald** (Bowen Basin mining services), **Gladstone**, **Townsville / Bohle**, **Toowoomba**, **Newcastle / Hunter**.
- Hook: "Mining services business in Mackay? Site numbers that match the accounts, every month." "Running sites across North Queensland? See every one of them on one screen."
- The Townsville margin-alert example from the landing page fits here directly.

### L3. "Brisbane North neighbours" (Qubik's backyard)
- Pins around **Strathpine, Brendale, North Lakes, Chermside, the Brisbane CBD and Fortitude Valley/Newstead**, radius 5–15 km.
- **A different offer:** "Accountants down the road in Strathpine. Come in for a coffee and a 30-minute look at what a live dashboard could show for your business." Meeting in person is something remote AI agencies can't match.
- Also run a LinkedIn version (LinkedIn can't target smaller than a city: use "Greater Brisbane").

### L4. Trade-show geofences (short bursts)
Pin the venue during the show and for 1–2 days after. Use event-themed hooks without implying we tracked anyone ("Planning your 2027 venue numbers?", never "We saw you at…").

| Event | Dates | Venue | Sector |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant & Foodservice Show (formerly Foodservice Australia) | 2–4 May 2027 | MCEC, Melbourne | Hospitality |
| Fine Food Australia | 6–9 Sep 2027 | ICC Sydney | Hospitality, food, retail |
| CeMAT Australia | July 2027 *(verify days)* | Melbourne *(verify)* | Logistics and warehousing |
| AIMEX | September 2027 *(verify days and venue)* | Sydney *(verify)* | Mining services |
| Early learning, allied health, vet and facilities conferences | *Research needed* | Various | Their sectors |

**Budget:** about A$30–50/day only on show days (+2 days after). Low total spend, and the venue only holds people from that industry for those days.

### L5. Office precincts for finance roles (low priority, test once)
- Brisbane CBD, North Sydney, Parramatta, Melbourne Southbank and St Kilda Road on weekday working hours, with CFO/finance hooks ("Still building the board pack by hand?").
- Weak on Meta because there's no job-title filter, but cheap to try for 2 weeks against the LinkedIn finance audience.

## 5. Signal-based targeting (timing is the targeting)

| Signal | How to spot it | Where it goes | Hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hiring admin** (accounts payable, data entry, payroll, reporting analyst, admin officer) | A weekly manual search of job boards (Seek, LinkedIn Jobs) for these roles at companies with 30+ staff in our sectors | Added to a "hiring" company list (LinkedIn) + Conversation Ad tree T2 + Dominic's note | "Hiring another accounts payable officer? Talk to us first." |
| **Opening new sites** | News, Google alerts, LinkedIn company posts, new licences in the liquor registers, new services on the ACECQA register | Tier upgrade + a personal note | "Opening site number 6? Your reporting has to scale with it." |
| **Using BI tools / spreadsheets** | LinkedIn skills targeting ("Power BI", "Excel modelling") + finance seniority; Meta interests in Power BI, Tableau, Xero, MYOB *(check availability)* | Its own LinkedIn and Meta ad sets | "Built a Power BI dashboard that nobody updates? We automate what feeds it." |
| **Fast growth** | Published fast-growth lists, funding news | Tier 1–2 | "Growing fast? Don't let admin grow with it." |
| **Ownership change / new CFO** | LinkedIn "new role" alerts in Sales Navigator for CFO/COO at list companies | Dominic's note within 30–60 days of the move | "Congratulations on the new role. A lot of CFOs start by fixing the reporting they inherited." |

## 6. Creative testing system

### Campaign structure
**Meta:**
| Campaign | Budget | Setup | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M-TEST** | A$25/day | One ad set per *concept* (3 ad versions each, different hooks); budget per ad set; Advantage+ audience; standard enhancements off so we know what we're testing | Find winning concepts |
| **M-SCALE** | From week 3–4 | Winning ads moved in **with the same post ID** (keeps the likes and comments); campaign-level budget | Spend more on what works |
| **M-LOCAL** | A$15/day | L1–L3 pin groups, one ad set per group | Location plays |
| **M-EVENT** | Bursts only | L4 | Trade shows |
| **M-RETARGET** | A$10/day | Site visitors, video viewers, form openers | Free call, prototype, calculator |

**LinkedIn:**
| Campaign | Budget | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| LI-1 Cold – Playbook Document Ad | A$25/day | Main cold lead source (base plan) |
| LI-2 Dominic Thought Leader Ads | A$12/day | Cheap reach, builds the warm pool |
| **LI-4 Qubik 500 – sector ads** | A$15/day | Sector-specific single images and video to the list companies × senior titles |
| **LI-5 Conversation Ads** | A$20/day | Trees 1–5 to list + warm audiences |
| LI-3 Retargeting – free call | A$10/day | Base plan |
| LI-TEST | uses LI-1's budget | 2–4 ads per campaign; LinkedIn's built-in A/B test for big questions (Document Ad vs video, for example) |

### What to test, in order
1. **Concept (angle + offer):** the big swings, e.g. key-person risk vs profit by site vs calculator vs accountants-not-AI-agency. Most of the difference in results comes from here.
2. **Hook** (first line or first 3 seconds) within the winning concept: 3–5 versions.
3. **Format:** the same winning message as video, static, carousel or text-style ad.
4. **Offer step:** playbook vs free call vs calculator vs mock-up vs lunch.

Test one level at a time. Don't change audience and creative together.

### Rules
- **Meta:** each concept gets about **A$120–150** (≈ 1.5–2× target cost per lead) before a decision. **Promote it** when the cost per lead is at or under target *and* at least 40% of leads qualify. **Kill it** when it has spent 2× target with no qualified lead, or when the 3-second view rate is under 25% (video) / link CTR is under 0.7%.
- **LinkedIn:** each ad gets about **A$300–500** or 5,000+ impressions before a decision. Kill below 0.4% CTR (single image) or under 10% form completion.
- **Creative fatigue:** on Meta, refresh when frequency passes ~3 in 7 days or cost per lead rises 30%+ over two weeks. On LinkedIn, refresh about every 4–6 weeks for the same audience.

### Creative formats to rotate (12)
1. **Dominic, talking to camera** (phone, 30–45s, captions burned in).
2. **Screen-recorded dashboard walk-through** with voice-over (sample data): "Here's a 12-venue group's Monday."
3. **Notification static:** a phone lock screen showing "Weekly profit report · Monday 7:00am" and "Margin alert · Townsville 4.1%".
4. **Spreadsheet vs dashboard:** a split image, the messy workbook on the left and the clean dashboard on the right.
5. **"Ugly" text ad:** plain text on a coloured background, looks like a post, not an ad. ("It's the 18th. Do you know last month's profit yet?")
6. **Carousel: "The 6 numbers"**, one number per card.
7. **Sector photo + call-out** ("Run 3+ clinics?") using the sector images we already have.
8. **Testimonial card:** "Dom has saved us more money than we've spent!" with a photo of Dominic.
9. **Long-form "letter from Dominic"** (LinkedIn especially): why an accountant builds AI dashboards.
10. **Relatable meme-style:** "Month-end, day 14" (the finance team buried in spreadsheets). Keep it light and on-brand.
11. **Whiteboard maths:** Dominic working through the calculator ("3 people × 10 hours × A$55 = …").
12. **Interactive:** a LinkedIn organic poll from Dominic ("How long after month-end do you see profit by site?"). Retarget everyone who voted.

### Hook bank (start here; 20 tested over time)
1. "If one person went on leave, would your reports still come out?"
2. "It's the 18th. Do you know last month's profit yet?"
3. "Which of your sites actually made money last month?"
4. "Every new site shouldn't mean another admin hire."
5. "3 people × 10 hours a week of copy-paste = A$75,900 a year."
6. "Most AI firms start with the technology. We start with your numbers."
7. "See a working prototype before you commit to the build."
8. "No new software. We connect the systems you already have."
9. "Your board is asking about AI. Start where it pays back."
10. "Wages crept to 38% at one venue. When did you find out?"
11. "Your busiest clinic might not be your most profitable."
12. "Do you know your margin per job, per depot, this week?"
13. "Which contracts are quietly losing money?"
14. "Occupancy is up. So why is the centre's profit down?"
15. "Doing the numbers on a Sunday night again?"
16. "Hiring another accounts payable officer? Talk to us first."
17. "Built a Power BI dashboard nobody updates?"
18. "Opening site number 6? Your reporting has to scale with it."
19. "Before 2027: get your numbers off spreadsheets."
20. "Accountants down the road in Strathpine. Come in for a coffee and a dashboard demo."

### Naming (so results map back to angles)
`CHANNEL_STAGE_AUDIENCE_CONCEPT_FORMAT_HOOK_v#`, for example `META_COLD_IND-BNE_A4-depot_VID-talk_H12_v1` or `LI_LIST_T1_A1-keyperson_DOC_H1_v2`.
The same text goes in `utm_content`, so every lead email names the exact ad.

### Production rhythm
- **Monthly:** one 2-hour shoot with Dominic → 10–12 clips (5 scripts × 2 takes + hooks re-recorded separately).
- **Weekly:** 3–5 new statics and carousels. **I can make these here** from HTML designs using our real dashboard screens, exported as PNG in the right sizes (1:1, 4:5, 9:16 for Meta; 1.91:1 and 1:1 for LinkedIn).
- **Weekly review (30 minutes, every Monday):** kill, promote, write the next week's briefs.

## 8. Timing plays

- **Month-end window:** run the "It's the 18th" and month-end hooks on **days 1–15 of each month**, when the pain is happening.
- **Sunday evening (Meta):** owner-operators doing the books at home. Schedule the "Sunday night" hook for Sat–Sun 6–10pm.
- **Now (mid-Nov to mid-Dec):** "Before 2027" planning hook. Budgets for next year are being set.
- **20 Dec – 10 Jan:** decision-makers are away, so reduce lead campaigns. Ads are cheaper then, so spend a little on **video views** to grow the retargeting pools for January.
- **January:** "New year, new numbers" push with the full budget.
- **April–June:** "Don't do another EOFY on spreadsheets."

**What's realistic:** with this mix, the list-based plays (Conversation Ads, mock-ups, the lunch) bring fewer leads but far better ones. Expect **3–8 qualified calls a month from the list work alone** once it's running (weeks 4+), on top of the base plan's cold leads. The 8-week test should end with **2–5 scoping sales across all channels**. That's an estimate, and depends heavily on Dominic's follow-up speed and on having one real case study.
