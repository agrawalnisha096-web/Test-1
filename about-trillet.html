The problem

High-stakes conversations are where automation keeps failing.

Every business has two kinds of conversations. The simple ones — a hold time, an FAQ, a status check. And the high-stakes ones: verifying who someone is, taking a payment, booking a critical appointment, handling a matter that's sensitive, regulated, or worth a great deal.

The first kind, most tools can handle. The second kind is where they fall apart. Generic voice bots can talk, but they can't verify, can't act inside your systems, and can't be trusted when compliance is on the line.

So the hardest, most valuable calls still land on already-stretched teams — or go unanswered. That gap is the whole reason Trillet exists.

Our story

Built by operators, engineers, and people who've run the phones.

Trillet didn't start in a lab. It started with founders who'd each spent years inside high-stakes conversations — from three different angles — and kept reaching the same conclusion: the technology wasn't good enough for the calls that mattered most.

01 — The operator

Someone who ran the contact centre.

Rob Petreski · CEO

Two decades running national contact centres and customer-experience teams across legal, government, and enterprise. He watched brilliant teams buried under volume, and every generation of automation break the moment a conversation got complicated.

He knew exactly where the bar had to be.

02 — The engineer

Someone who built the critical systems.

Laurence Latin · Co-Founder & CTO

Fifteen years building mission-critical, security-sensitive software — including for healthcare, where "mostly works" isn't good enough. He understood what it truly takes to verify an identity and act inside a live business system.

He knew how to make it hold up under scrutiny.

03 — The builder

Someone who'd scaled voice AI.

Ming Xu · Co-Founder & COO

He'd already scaled a voice-AI consultancy to seven-figure revenue as one of the earliest partners of a leading voice-AI platform — seeing both how powerful the technology was becoming and exactly where it kept letting businesses down.

He knew what was possible, and what wasn't yet.

Put those three perspectives in a room and the product designs itself. Trillet is what you get when the person who's run the contact centre, the person who's built the critical systems, and the person who's scaled the voice AI decide to build the thing none of them could buy.
